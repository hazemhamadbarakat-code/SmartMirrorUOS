Serial protocol

Uno → Pi

  • RDY:UNO:1Sent at boot and in reply to PING.
  • EVT:MOTION:1PIR went high.
  • EVT:MOTION:05 s with no motion. Presence, not occupancy.
  • EVT:HELPDebounced press, repeat-locked for 2 s.
  • HB:<millis>Heartbeat every 10 s, so the Pi can spot a dead link.
  • ERR:<text>Command the Uno could not parse.

Pi → Uno

  • LED:RED:ONColours RED, AMBER, GREEN, ALL. Values ON, OFF, BLINK.
  • BUZZ:300One beep in milliseconds, 1 to 5000, non-blocking.
  • PINGLiveness check.

Bench test order

Each step proves one thing. Do not skip ahead when one fails.

  1. Power and LEDs only

    Wire the three LEDs and resistors. Upload, open Serial Monitor at 115200. Green should light and RDY:UNO:1 should print.

  2. Command the LEDs from the host

    Send LED:AMBER:BLINK, then LED:ALL:OFF. If nothing happens, check the Serial Monitor line ending is set to Newline.

  3. HELP button

    Add the button between D5 and GND. One press prints EVT:HELP once, the red LED blinks and the buzzer chirps. Multiple lines per press means the debounce wiring is loose, not the code.

  4. Buzzer driver

    Send BUZZ:1000. If the Uno resets when it sounds, the buzzer is loading the pin directly: fit the NPN and the base resistor.

  5. PIR, after warm-up

    Power the PIR, then leave the bench for a full minute. The HC-SR501 self-calibrates and its output is meaningless until it settles. Then walk in: EVT:MOTION:1, and EVT:MOTION:0 about 5 s after you stop moving.

  6. Trim the PIR pots

    Set sensitivity and the on-board time delay to the shortest usable values, so the sketch controls the hold time rather than the module. Record the final pot positions before anything is mounted in the frame.

Simulate it

Two files in the project folder run the whole step in a browser.

  1. Open a blank Uno project at wokwi.com

    Choose Arduino Uno. The editor gives you sketch.ino and a diagram tab.

  2. Paste both files

    Sketch body from smartmirror_uno_step1.ino, and the diagram tab from diagram.json.

  3. Run

    Click the PIR to toggle motion, click the button for HELP, watch the LEDs and the serial pane. You can type commands such as LED:RED:BLINK into the simulated serial monitor.

The simulated buzzer is wired straight to D11 because Wokwi models an ideal load. The physical build keeps the NPN driver shown in the pin map.

Before you power it

Never wire Uno pins to Raspberry Pi GPIO.

The Uno runs 5 V logic and the Pi's GPIO pins are 3.3 V only. Connecting them damages the Pi. The two boards talk over the USB-A to USB-B data cable and nothing else.

Check LED polarity before power.

Long leg is the anode and goes to the Uno pin; short leg is the cathode and goes through the resistor to ground. One resistor per LED, not one shared resistor.

The PIR output is 5 V and the module needs 5 V in.

Feed it from the Uno 5 V pin while bench testing. Its output stays high for its own on-board delay, which is why the sketch adds its own 5 s hold rather than trusting the edge.

Breadboard now, perfboard later.

Hold off on soldering until steps 1 and 2 both pass. Nothing on a breadboard goes behind a wall-mounted mirror.
